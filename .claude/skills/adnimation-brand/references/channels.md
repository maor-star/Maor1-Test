# Channels — specs, cadence, rhythm

## LinkedIn

4–5 posts per week, Monday to Thursday mornings ET.

- 80–200 words
- First line stands alone and says something specific. It is read without the rest;
  a hook that only makes sense after the click is a wasted line
- Ends on a statement, not a generic question
- Maximum three hashtags
- Exactly one audience, tagged in the front matter

### The weekly mix

| Slot | Per week |
|---|---|
| Professional insight | 1–2 |
| Point of view | 1 |
| Success story | 1 |
| What the company actually does | biweekly |
| Announcements and event content | as they arise |

A week that hits four of five slots with real material beats a week that hits five
by padding one. If a slot cannot be filled honestly — most often the success story,
which needs real numbers — leave it empty and say why in the calendar.

## Blog

2–3 posts per month, 1,200–2,000 words.

- Defined search intent, stated before writing
- One primary keyword, 3–5 secondary
- Hierarchical H2/H3
- Meta description under 155 characters
- 2–4 internal links
- **At least one thing the reader can apply without hiring Adnimation**

That last requirement is the whole difference between an article and a brochure.
Some readers will take the method and never call. That is the cost of being worth
reading, and it is worth paying — the ones who do call arrive already believing you
know the problem.

## Newsletter

Biweekly Tuesday.

1. Short first-person opener
2. 2–3 market updates, each with the publisher implication spelled out rather than
   left implied
3. One blog article
4. One practical tip
5. One clear CTA — one, not a primary and a secondary

Routine issues publish independently. The market updates require an actual market
review; if there are no confirmed monitoring sources, leave those slots empty and
say so rather than inventing movements to fill them.

## Comments

5–10 per week on publisher and industry content. Two to four sentences that add
information. Never promotional — no product mention, no link, no "we see this all
the time" leading into a pitch.

Agreement with elaboration is fine; agreement alone is noise. Disagreement is
allowed; naming and attacking a competitor is not — argue the position, never the
company.

Prefer commenting on the original post over publishing a parallel one. On a topic a
monitored source raised, a good comment beats a competing post and does not trip the
48-hour rule.

The highest-value comment available costs nothing: when someone asserts a benchmark
without stating its basis, ask what the basis is, neutrally.

## Operating rhythm

| Cadence | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Sunday** | Market review, build the week's content calendar, submit approval items |
| **Daily** | Publish per calendar, comment round, monitor recent post performance |
| **Monthly** | One-page performance report, next month's calendar, proposed blog topics |
| **Quarterly** | Message audit — what worked, whether the pillars still hold |

### The monthly one-pager

1. **What published** — count by slot type against the weekly mix, and every gap
   with its reason
2. **What performed** — top three and bottom three, one sentence on what separates
   them. With no analytics access, say so rather than estimating
3. **Audience split achieved** versus the 60/25/15 target. Drift here is the
   earliest signal the programme is narrowing
4. **Approval throughput** — submitted, cleared, average wait. If drafts sit for a
   week the cadence is fiction regardless of how much gets written
5. **What stayed blocked** and what it cost
6. **Next month's calendar and three proposed blog topics**

### The quarterly message audit

1. Which pillar produced the content that actually landed, and does the priority
   order still match?
2. Has the market adopted any term we introduced? "Agentic yield layer" is the test
   case — if nobody repeats it within two quarters, it is our phrase and not a
   category, and that is worth knowing early
3. What did we assert repeatedly without ever proving? That is next quarter's
   research, not next quarter's copy
4. Where did we sound like everyone else? Specific posts, not a feeling

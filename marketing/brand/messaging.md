# Messaging Reference

## Positioning

Adnimation built an agentic AI layer that learns the site, the audience, and actual
user behavior, builds data layers the standard stack does not see, and runs AI agents
that push pricing upward in real time.

Others sell access to a platform. Here the technology does the work and the team runs it.

**Category descriptor, first mention in any text:** "agentic yield layer"

**Product name:** _Adnimation Lift_ — **PENDING approval and trademark clearance.**
Do not use in any published asset until cleared. Drafts referencing the product use
the category descriptor instead.

## Message pillars, in priority order

Every piece of content maps to one. If it maps to none, it does not get written.

1. **The agentic AI layer.** Never the generic "we use AI" — that means nothing in
   2026. Always three specifics: it learns this particular property and its audience;
   it builds data layers above what the standard stack sees; it runs agents that act
   on pricing continuously rather than on fixed rules.
2. **Proven impact.** The ~50% average improvement, always with its basis. See the
   claim rules below — currently blocked.
3. **Active management, not just tooling.** Technology nobody runs is another dashboard.
4. **Revenue transparency.** The publisher sees what comes in and what is taken.
5. **User experience and long-term partnership.** Speed, Core Web Vitals, and ad
   density are part of revenue over time, not opposed to it.

## Audiences

One per post. Tag it in the post's front matter.

**1. Publishers (~60%).** Content site operators, small and mid-sized media networks,
app developers, in-house monetization managers, primarily US. Owns ad revenue, has no
full ad ops team. Technically literate, allergic to marketing language. Cares about:
revenue, effort required, who is actually behind it.

**2. Demand and supply partners (~25%).** SSPs, DSPs, networks, direct advertisers,
inventory sources, technology partners. Cares about: inventory quality, scale,
transparency, campaign performance, absence of problematic traffic, payment
reliability, long-term seriousness. Not impressed by publisher-facing claims.

**3. Brand and company (~15%).** People, events, culture, what the company actually does.

A post that addresses all three addresses none.

## Technology disclosure

Say WHAT. Say the RESULT. Never say HOW.

**Permitted:** that the system learns the property, the audience, and user behavior;
that it builds data layers the standard stack lacks; that AI agents act on pricing in
real time and continuously; what this gives a publisher (higher prices, no work on
their side, no damage to experience); what it gives a demand partner (inventory with
deeper understanding of the audience behind it).

**Never disclosed:** architecture, models, data sources, signals, training method,
pricing logic. No technical "how it works" articles. No deep-dive mechanism posts.

Interest is sustained by going **deep on the problem** and **precise on the result**.
A post explaining why static floor pricing leaves money on the table is excellent
content and reveals nothing.

## The 50% claim

> **STATUS: BLOCKED — cannot be used in any asset yet.**
> The approved wording requires four facts not currently on file. See
> `approvals/queue.md` item Q1. Until they are supplied, no draft may carry the
> figure in any form.

Rules, once unblocked:

- Always state the basis: measured against the client's previous stack, across N
  properties, over a stated period. Never a bare "up to 50%".
- It is a **historical average**, explicitly not a forecast for a prospective client.
- Prefer a range: "averaging roughly 50%, ranging X to Y across properties".
- **One approved wording, identical everywhere.** Never round up. Never write "more
  than 50%". Never generate variants.

Approved wording, to be filled once the facts are confirmed and then frozen:

```
Across [N] properties measured against each publisher's previous stack over
[PERIOD], revenue improvement averaged roughly 50%, ranging [X]% to [Y]% by
property. That is a historical average, not a projection for any new publisher.
```

## Banned language

revolutionary · game-changer · unlock · supercharge · "in today's fast-paced digital
landscape" · leverage · synergy · cutting-edge · emoji bullet points · one-line hooks
followed by whitespace padding

## Voice

Confident, direct, professional. Written to a peer, not a prospect. Short sentences.
Specific numbers when we have them. Trade-offs acknowledged. No manufactured enthusiasm.

## Never published, under any framing

- The identity of the Asian market partner, ownership structure, profit split, staged
  terms, qualification thresholds, volume targets, or dates. Expansion into Asian
  markets may be discussed in general terms with prior approval; the deal itself
  never. Nothing about Asia publishes before the activity is actually live.
- Company revenue, margins, or fee structure
- Compensation or personnel matters
- Legal, regulatory, or privacy compliance claims
- Any negative or comparative mention of a named competitor
- Guaranteed numerical outcomes for prospective publishers
- Responses to press inquiries or public client complaints — escalate immediately

## Fabrication line

Never invent client names, statistics, partner relationships, or product
capabilities. If a fact is missing, flag it and ask. This applies especially to how
the technology works, to partner relationships, and to any number. Ask rather than
approximate.

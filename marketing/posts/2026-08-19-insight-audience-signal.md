## Copy

Most audience signal on a bid request is an inference drawn from a URL.

A buyer gets a domain, a path, a handful of standard fields, and works out the rest.
Sometimes the inference is good. On a large content site with twelve unrelated sections
it is not — the same domain carries a reader comparing mortgage rates and a reader
looking at photos of dogs, and the bid request describes them identically.

That gap costs both sides. The buyer overpays for one impression and underpays for the
other, averages the result, and concludes the inventory is mediocre. The publisher sees
a low clearing price and concludes the buyer is not serious. Both are reading the same
missing information and blaming each other for it.

What a demand partner should be asking supply for is not more segments. It is evidence
that the seller understands the audience behind a specific placement well enough for the
price to mean something, and a clear account of what is being measured to get there.

That is a reasonable thing to ask for. It is also a short list of sellers who can answer it.
